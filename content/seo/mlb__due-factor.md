## Run To Your Bets MLB Due Factor

The Due Factor tracks how every MLB market is running against its own season rate: NRFI / YRFI in the 1st inning; the moneyline (home or away, favorite or dog), the run line and the total for the First 5; the same for the full game. Each market counts one side against the other (Over against Under, Home against Away, Favorites against Dogs), and only games with a line for it; a market with no lines counts results and says so.

### How to read it

Pick the view and the season (this one or last), then the segment and the market. The segments run 1st Inning and First 5, then the Full Run: Full Game; the Full Run segments carry a gold dot. One market shows at a time.

- **Daily:** the last 10 game days, newest first: games played, each side's count and the first side's %, with a Last 10 days total. The summary line compares the last 7 days with the season.
- **Weekly:** one row per Monday-Sunday week of the season, with a Season total. The summary line compares this week.
- **Monthly:** a calendar of the month. Each day shows its record for the market and is shaded by the side that won it (the legend under the grid names them); a dashed day had no games. Click a day for that day's matchups, and use the arrows to move through the season's months.
- **Summary line:** the period's record and % for the first side, the season's, and the gap in points. Fewer than 15 graded games in the period reads Small sample. Early in a season, until 100 games are graded, it compares with last season's rate.
- **Tint:** a % cell 10+ points off the season rate is shaded for the side running hot.

### Example

Say NRFI has hit 56% of the time over the last 7 days, against 49% for the season. That is 7 points over, so the summary line ends with +7 pts. Hot means the league is running over its season rate. It is not a sign that a side is about to hit: every game is its own bet.

### What I look for

A big gap on a handful of games is noise. I want the gap to hold for a week or more before I read anything into it, and I check the price before I bet any side.

## FAQ

### What is the MLB Due Factor?

A hot and cold tracker. It compares each MLB first inning & first five market over the last 7 days with its season rate, postseason included, and shows the gap in points.

### Does a cold market mean it is due?

No. Cold means the market is running under its season rate right now. Past results don't change the odds of the next game.

### Which games count?

Regular season and postseason games with a line for the market (a market with no lines counts every result), graded at the closing line. Pushes are left out, and ties are left out of the moneyline rates.
