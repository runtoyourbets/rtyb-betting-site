## Run To Your Bets College Basketball Due Factor

The Due Factor tracks how the College Basketball first half markets are running against their own season rate. Each market is measured on one side: the first half Spread (home team and favorite against the spread), Total (Over) and Moneyline (home team, ties out). The gap is the rate over the last 7 days minus the season rate, in points, and the cards are ordered by the biggest gap.

### How to read it

- **On pace:** under 5 points from the season rate.
- **Leaning hot / Leaning cold:** 5 to 9 points above or below.
- **Hot / Cold:** 10 points or more.

The Daily view adds last night's results, a day-by-day table and, on game days, the coldest side on today's games with the best price. The Weekly view shows every week of the regular season. A table cell 10+ points above its season rate is tinted.

### Example

Say the first half Over has hit 48% of the time over the last 7 days, against 60% for the season. That is 12 points under the season rate, so the Total card reads Cold. Cold means the league is running under its season rate. It is not a sign that a side is about to hit: every game is its own bet.

### What I look for

A big gap on a handful of games is noise. I want the gap to hold for a week or more before I read anything into it, and I check the price before I bet any side.

## FAQ

### What is the College Basketball Due Factor?

A hot and cold tracker. It compares each College Basketball first half market over the last 7 days with its season rate, regular season only, and shows the gap in points.

### Does a cold market mean it is due?

No. Cold means the market is running under its season rate right now. Past results don't change the odds of the next game.

### Which games count?

Regular season games with a line for the market, graded at the closing line. Pushes are left out, and ties are left out of the moneyline rates.
