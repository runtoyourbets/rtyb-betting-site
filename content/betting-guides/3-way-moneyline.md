If you've bet soccer, you know this one. A 3-way moneyline gives you three choices: Team A, Team B or Tie. In soccer it's called a draw; in hockey it's a tie. On Run To Your Bets you'll see it on the NHL first period.

## How it works

- **Team A** wins if Team A is ahead after the first period.
- **Team B** wins if Team B is ahead.
- **Tie** wins if it's level. If it's tied, Team A and Team B both lose.

That's why the prices on Team A and Team B are better than on the 2-way moneyline. On the 2-way, a tie is a push and you get your money back. On the 3-way, a tie is a loss unless you bet the tie.

## How often first periods end tied

This season, NHL first periods have ended tied {{nhl.1p.tie:season}} of the time. That's a lot of games where a 2-way bet pushes and a 3-way bet loses.

## Playing the tie

If you play the tie, you're usually hoping for a 0-0 or a 1-1. Here's the thing: the most common first period tie this season isn't 0-0. It's 1-1, slightly more often.

So if 1-1 is the score you're hoping for, you might be better off playing Both Teams to Score. It's going to be a plus money return anyway, and BTTS Yes also wins on 2-1, 3-1 and every other score where both teams get one. [BTTS explained](/betting-guides/btts/)

## 2-way or 3-way?

- **2-way:** less payout, but a tie gives you your money back.
- **3-way:** more payout on your team, but a tie loses.

If you think it's going to be close after 20 minutes, the 2-way's push is worth paying for. If you think your team is clearly better early, the 3-way pays more for the same pick.

## FAQ

### What does 3-way moneyline mean in hockey?
Three outcomes: Team A, Team B or Tie. If the first period ends level, only the tie wins.

### Is a 3-way moneyline the same as soccer betting?
Yes, the same idea. Soccer calls the third option a draw; hockey calls it a tie.

### What happens to a 3-way bet on Team A if the period ends tied?
It loses. Only the tie wins on a tied period.
