Both Teams to Score is exactly what it sounds like. BTTS Yes wins if both teams score in the first period. BTTS No wins if at least one team gets shut out. On Run To Your Bets you'll see it on the NHL first period.

## BTTS vs the Over 1.5

They're not the same bet. A 2-0 first period wins the Over 1.5 but loses BTTS Yes. BTTS needs a goal from each team.

That's why it's harder. This season BTTS Yes has hit {{nhl.1p.btts_yes:season}} in the first period, against {{nhl.1p.over:season}} for the Over 1.5. And that's why BTTS Yes pays plus money.

## When I play BTTS Yes

- A good offensive team on the road, against a home team I trust to show up and get at least one.
- A decent underdog walking into a home team that can score.

The best spots have two teams that can both score and neither one can really keep the other out.

## BTTS instead of the tie

If you're thinking about the tie on the 3-way and hoping for a 1-1, play BTTS Yes instead. It's a plus money return anyway, and it also wins on 2-1, 3-1 and every score where both teams get one. 1-1 is the most common first period tie this season. ## BTTS No

BTTS No wins on 0-0, 1-0, 2-0, 3-0. It's the side that usually hits, so it's priced like a favorite. It's a good fit for two teams that rarely score early, or one team that's great at keeping opponents off the board in the first.

## FAQ

### What does BTTS mean in hockey?
Both Teams to Score. BTTS Yes wins if both teams score in the period; BTTS No wins if at least one team doesn't.

### Is BTTS the same as Over 1.5?
No. A 2-0 period is an Over but not a BTTS Yes.

### Does BTTS count overtime?
Not on a first period bet. Only the first 20 minutes count.
