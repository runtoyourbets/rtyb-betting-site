NHL first period betting is one of my favorite bets, and I keep it simple: Overs and Both Teams to Score. The first period is 20 minutes, it settles in about 35 to 40 minutes, and you're not sweating an empty-net goal in the third.

## The bets

### Moneyline (2-way)

Pick who's winning after 20 minutes. If it's tied, it's a push and you get your money back. That's the advantage: you can't lose on a tie.

### Total (Over/Under 1.5)

Sportsbooks always post the first period total at 1.5 goals. Many also offer alternate lines from 0.5 to 2.5 and higher, but the base line is always 1.5. Get two goals and you've got a winner. That's the way I look at it.

Because the base line doesn't move, the books move the price instead. A game with a 6.5 total gets a more expensive Over than a 5.5 game. So when you see the 1P Over at -160 one night and -125 the next, that's the game total talking.

Unders? They're tough to watch. I've seen plenty of games go 1-0 in the first two minutes and then nothing the rest of the period.

### Both Teams to Score (BTTS)

Yes means both teams score in the period; No means at least one gets shut out. It's harder than the Over. This season BTTS Yes has hit {{nhl.1p.btts_yes:season}}, which is why it pays plus money.

When I play it: a good offensive team on the road, and a home team I trust to show up and get at least one. Or a decent underdog walking into a home team that can score.

### 3-Way Moneyline

If you've bet soccer, you know this one. It's the same three choices, except in hockey the draw is called a tie: Team A, Team B or Tie. If it's level after the period, Team A and Team B both lose. That's why the prices are better than the 2-way.

Thinking about the tie? The most common first-period tie isn't 0-0, it's 1-1. If 1-1 is the score you're hoping for, play BTTS Yes instead. You get paid on 1-1, and on every 2-1 and 3-1 after it.

## How it settles

- Only the first 20 minutes count. What happens after that doesn't matter.
- A tied first period is a push on the 2-way moneyline and a winner only for the tie on the 3-way.
- The 1.5 total can't push: there's no such thing as one and a half goals.
- If the game is postponed, first-period bets are void. Once the period is played, the bet stands, whatever happens later.

## Why the first period is different

More goals come later in the game. The first period has only about {{nhl.1p.goal_share:season}} of all goals.

The second period has the long change: each team's bench is farthest from the net it's defending, so tired players take longer to get off the ice, and that's where odd-man rushes come from. In the third, teams pull the goalie earlier than ever, and that means empty-net chances. The first period has none of that. Teams feel each other out, and the scoring is tighter.

## What moves the first period

- **The starting goalies.** Check who's starting before you bet. A backup in net changes the first period more than anything.
- **The game total.** It sets the price on the 1P Over. A 6.5 game is a better Over spot than a 5.5.
- **Rest.** Teams on a back-to-back can start flat. Rest is on every team page, by days off.
- **Home ice.** Home teams get the last change. It helps, but less than people think: home teams have won the first period {{nhl.1p.home_ml:season}} this season.
- **Who scores first.** A team that usually scores first against a team that usually gives one up early is a BTTS No or Under spot to watch.

## Common mistakes

- Playing the tie hoping for 1-1. That's a BTTS Yes bet that also pays on 2-1.
- Paying a big price for a home favorite in the first period. A 2-way ML at -150 for a period that ends tied a third of the time is a lot to lay.
- Betting the first period without checking the starting goalies.
- Forgetting that the 2-way moneyline pushes on a tie. It's insurance, but it's not a win.

## FAQ

### Does overtime count in a first period bet?
No. Only the first 20 minutes count. Overtime and shootouts don't matter.

### What happens to a first period moneyline bet if the period ends tied?
On a 2-way moneyline it's a push and you get your stake back. On a 3-way moneyline, only the tie wins; home and away lose.

### Why is the first period total always 1.5?
Most first periods have one or two goals, so 1.5 splits them best. Books keep the base line at 1.5 and move the price instead, based on the full-game total. Many also offer alternate lines from 0.5 to 2.5 and higher.

### Is BTTS the same as the Over 1.5?
No. A 2-0 period wins the Over but loses BTTS Yes. BTTS needs a goal from each team.
