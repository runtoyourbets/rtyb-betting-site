A unit is a standard bet size. Instead of saying "I won $500," bettors say "I'm up 5 units," so it means the same thing whether you bet $10 a game or $1,000. On Run To Your Bets, every record with a price shows units.

## How Run To Your Bets counts units

- 1 unit bet on every game.
- At the closing price, the last price before the game started.
- A win at -110 is +0.91u. A win at +150 is +1.5u. A loss is -1u. A push is 0.

So +6.4u means if you'd bet the same amount on every one of those games, you'd be up 6.4 times that amount.

When you see a dash instead of units, we don't have enough saved prices for that team or market yet.

## Risk it or win it

Every bettor has to decide how they size a bet. Are you risking one unit, or betting enough to win one unit? Say your unit is $50:

| Price | Risk $50, you win | To win $50, you risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -120 | $41.67 | $60 |
| -110 | $45.45 | $55 |
| +150 | $75 | $33.33 |

So a "to win" bettor risks $60 at -120 to win $50. A "risk" bettor puts $50 down and wins $41.67. Pick one and stick with it.

Run To Your Bets counts units the risk way: one unit risked on every game.

## Why units matter more than win %

Win percentage doesn't know the price. A favorite at -200 can win 60% of the time and still lose money. An underdog at +150 can win 45% of the time and make money.

Units count the price. That's why every Run To Your Bets record shows both: the % first, then the record, then the units.

## How to size a unit

Most bettors keep a unit at 1% to 5% of their bankroll. The point is to bet the same amount every time, so one bad night doesn't wreck you.

## FAQ

### What does +3.5u mean?
Up three and a half units. Bet $100 a game and you'd be up $350.

### Why does Run To Your Bets grade at the closing price?
It's the last price before the game starts, the same price for everyone. It's the fairest way to compare.

### Why do some teams show a dash for units?
We don't have enough saved prices for that team or market yet.
