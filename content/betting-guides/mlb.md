Baseball's early bets come down to one thing: the starting pitchers. The first inning is starter vs starter against the top of each lineup. The first five innings is starter vs starter before the bullpens show up. A first inning bet settles in about 15 to 20 minutes, the first five in about an hour and a half, sometimes sooner if the pitchers are humming.

## The bets

### NRFI and YRFI

NRFI is No Run First Inning; YRFI is Yes Run First Inning. Most people say them "NURF-ee" and "YUR-fee." NRFI wins if nobody scores in the 1st. YRFI wins if anybody does, top or bottom.

This is starters vs starters. Each pitcher faces the first five hitters, maybe six. Get one or two guys on base and you've got a runner in scoring position. So can those top five hitters get one run across against that starter? That's the whole bet.

Look at ERA, WHIP and strikeout rate. The bullpen has nothing to do with it, so the lineups matter more than ever. Heavy offense against a bad pitcher leans YRFI. A good pitcher against a bad offense leans NRFI. Nothing's guaranteed, though. A good pitcher can give up one run on a mistake pitch and then go scoreless the rest of the night.

It's close to a coin flip: {{mlb.1i.nrfi:season}} NRFI. So the price is everything.

### First Five (F5) moneyline

Pick who's leading after five full innings. It's still mostly about the starters, but the manager matters too. How long is the leash? Will he pull his guy early if he gets in trouble? A 1, 2 or 3 starter usually gets a higher pitch count and goes deeper.

Every lineup gets at least two looks at the starter in five innings. So do you trust those nine hitters more than the pitcher, or the pitcher more than the nine hitters? That's how you bet the first five.

A tie after five is common, {{mlb.f5.tie:season}} of games, so check how your book settles it. Most books push a 2-way F5 moneyline on a tie.

### F5 run line

Baseball's spread for the first five, usually half a run. The favorite at -0.5 has to be ahead after five. The underdog at +0.5 wins if it's ahead or tied. That half run is why the run line exists: it turns all those ties into a winner for the dog.

### F5 total

Over or under on the combined runs after five innings. Same thinking as the first inning, stretched out: two starters, two lineups, two times through the order.

Books start with the full game total. A 7.5 or 8 game usually gets a first five total of 3.5 to 4. Go higher, a 9 or 10, and you're looking at 5, sometimes higher. It all depends on the starters and the lineups.

## How it settles

- First inning bets settle after the bottom of the 1st. Nothing after that counts.
- First five bets need five full innings. If the game is stopped before that, they're usually void. Check your book's rules.
- A tie after five pushes a 2-way F5 moneyline and goes to the +0.5 side on the run line.

## Every ballpark is different

That's what baseball is all about. It's not like any other sport. The bases and the mound are the same everywhere, but the fences, the foul territory and the air are not. It's not an equal playing field.

In 2026, the first inning stayed scoreless just 28% of the time at Coors Field in Colorado. In Baltimore it was 63%. Texas (40%) and the Athletics (42%) were on the low end; San Diego and Detroit (60%) were up top. That's a bigger swing than most pitcher matchups.

## What moves the first inning and the first five

- **The starters.** ERA, WHIP, strikeouts, and how they've looked lately.
- **The top of the lineup.** The first five hitters decide the first inning.
- **Lefty or righty.** Always check the lineup against the starter's throwing hand.
- **The ballpark.** Coors and Baltimore are night and day.
- **Weather.** Wind blowing out, heat, humidity: they all move the ball.
- **Day or night.** Day games after night games can mean tired lineups and rested regulars sitting.
- **The leash.** For the first five, how deep does this manager let this starter go?

## Common mistakes

- Betting NRFI on a name. A good pitcher can still give one up on a mistake pitch.
- Forgetting the bottom of the 1st. YRFI wins on a run from either team.
- Playing the F5 moneyline when you really want the dog. If you like the underdog to hang around, +0.5 on the run line also pays on a tie.
- Ignoring the ballpark.

## FAQ

### What does NRFI mean?
No Run First Inning. It wins if neither team scores in the 1st inning. YRFI, Yes Run First Inning, is the other side.

### How do you say NRFI and YRFI?
Most bettors say "NURF-ee" and "YUR-fee."

### Do bullpens matter for first five bets?
Mostly no. First five bets are built around the starters. The bullpen only matters if a starter gets pulled early.

### What happens to a first five bet if the game is rained out?
If five full innings aren't played, most books void first five bets. Check your book's rules.

### What happens if the first five ends tied?
A 2-way F5 moneyline usually pushes. On the F5 run line, the +0.5 side wins.
