NBA first quarter and first half bets are all about doing the math, then adding a bucket. Books start with the full game line and cut it up. Once you know how they cut it, you know what you're looking at. A first quarter bet settles in about 30 to 35 minutes; a first half in about an hour.

## How books cut the game line

### Spreads

A team favored by 3 or 4 for the game is usually laying half a point to a point in the first quarter, and about half the game number in the first half. It's just the way it is.

### Totals

You'd think a 240 game has a 60-point first quarter. It doesn't. Books take the game total, divide by four, and add a point and a half to two and a half. On a 240 game you'll see a first quarter around 61.5 or 62.5.

The first half works the same way. On a 240 game, expect 121 or 122, not 120. Every now and then you'll see it under half, a 119.5 or 119, because of injuries, the way a book sets it up, or a team's tendencies.

Through Nov 19 this season, the typical NBA first quarter total sat 1.6 points above a straight quarter of the game total, and the first half 2.8 points above a straight half.

### Moneyline

First quarter moneylines on a small favorite usually land around -120 to -130. Home teams are favored more often than not.

## The bets

- **1Q / 1H spread:** the favorite has to win the quarter or half by more than the number. [Spread explained](/betting-guides/spread/)
- **1Q / 1H total:** the combined points in the quarter or half, over or under. [Totals explained](/betting-guides/totals/)
- **1Q / 1H moneyline:** who's ahead at the end of the quarter or half. A tie usually pushes. [Moneyline explained](/betting-guides/moneyline/)

## How it settles

- A first quarter bet settles at the end of the 1st quarter; a first half bet at halftime.
- Overtime never counts toward a first half bet.
- A tie on a 2-way moneyline pushes; a whole-number spread or total that lands right on the number pushes.

## Depth is key

Some teams come out swinging and fade. In mid-November the Bucks were 6-9 in first quarters, 3-12 at the half and 1-14 by the final buzzer. They could hang early; they didn't have the depth to finish. That's exactly the kind of team where the first quarter and the full game tell two different stories.

## What moves the first quarter and first half

- **Starters.** The first quarter is mostly starters. Check who's in and who's out before you bet.
- **Depth.** The first half brings in the bench. A team with a thin bench can win the first quarter and lose the half.
- **Rest.** Back-to-backs and long road trips show up early. Rest is on every team page, by days off.
- **Pace.** Fast teams push first quarter totals; slow teams sit on them.
- **Home court.** It helps. Home teams are favored most nights for a reason.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing the game total by four and calling it a day. The first quarter line is usually a point or two higher.
- Betting the first half like it's the first quarter. The bench plays in the second quarter.
- Ignoring late injury news. A starter sitting changes the first quarter more than the full game.

## FAQ

### Does overtime count in a first half bet?
No. A first half bet is settled at halftime. Overtime only affects full game bets.

### How are NBA first quarter totals set?
Books start with the game total, divide by four and usually add 1.5 to 2.5 points. A 240 game gets a first quarter around 61.5 to 62.5.

### What happens if the first quarter ends tied?
A 2-way moneyline pushes and you get your money back. On a 3-way, only the tie wins. Playing the tie on an NBA 3-way is rare, almost jackpot seeking. It doesn't happen often; you're better off betting a game to go to overtime.

### Is the first half spread half the game spread?
Close. It's usually about half the full game spread, rounded to a half point.
