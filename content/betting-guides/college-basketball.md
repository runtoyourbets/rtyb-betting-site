College basketball is played in two 20-minute halves, so the early bet here is the first half. It settles in about an hour. And it's a different animal from the NBA: the books shade the first half total down, not up.

## How books cut the game line

### Spreads

The first half spread is usually about half the game spread, give or take half a point.

### Totals

Say you've got a 150 game total. You'd think the first half would be 75. It won't be. Books shade it down. Through Nov 19 this season, the typical college first half total sat 3.8 points under half the game total, so a 150 game gets about a 71.

Why? The second half has the fouls, the free throws and the late-game clock stoppages. The first half doesn't. In college, the clock doesn't stop on a made basket; it only stops on a whistle. I've heard plenty of bettors say you're really only playing a 19-minute first half.

So if you're on the Under of a 75-point first half and it's sitting at 68 or 70 going into the last minute, you should get a W. There's only going to be one, maybe two possessions for each team. That's just the max.

### Moneyline

Home teams are favored in most college games, and the big ones get heavy first half moneylines.

## The bets

- **1H spread:** the favorite has to win the half by more than the number. [Spread explained](/betting-guides/spread/)
- **1H total:** the combined points at halftime, over or under. [Totals explained](/betting-guides/totals/)
- **1H moneyline:** who's ahead at halftime. A tie usually pushes. [Moneyline explained](/betting-guides/moneyline/)

## How it settles

- First half bets settle at halftime. Nothing in the second half or overtime counts.
- A tie on a 2-way moneyline pushes; a whole-number line that lands right on the number pushes.

## What moves the first half

- **Mismatches.** Early in the season it's a lot of big programs against small ones. Big spreads, heavy moneylines.
- **Pace.** Tempo decides the first half total more than anything.
- **Home court.** It matters more in college than in the pros: loud gyms, young players.
- **The last minute.** Teams hold for the last shot. Expect one or two possessions, not five.

## Common mistakes

- Cutting the game total in half. The first half line is usually a few points lower, and for good reason.
- Counting on a late flurry. The first half doesn't end like the second half; there's no fouling to stop the clock.

## FAQ

### Why is the college basketball first half total less than half the game total?
The second half has more fouls, free throws and clock stoppages. Books shade the first half down, by about 3.8 points on average this season.

### Does the clock stop on made baskets in college basketball?
Not in the first half. The clock only stops on a made basket in the last minute of the second half and in overtime.

### Does overtime count in a first half bet?
No. First half bets settle at halftime.
